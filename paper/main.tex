\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A classifier accurate at deployment does not stay so: its input distribution
keeps changing, a main source of technical debt in production machine learning
\cite{sculley2015debt}. Monitoring for this kind
of \emph{concept drift} is difficult for one reason: the labels that would
establish whether the model is still correct arrive late, or not at all
\cite{gama2014survey, lu2018learning}. Practitioners therefore rely on
label-free proxies. One family monitors the input distribution and alarms on
any change, which overwhelms the operator with alerts about shifts the model
absorbs without loss \cite{rabanser2019failing}. A newer family monitors the
model's \emph{outputs}: confidence, entropy, or learned error proxies
(Sect.~\ref{sec:related}).

Our starting point is one structural fact about that second family. Any
statistic computed from the outputs is blind to any change that leaves them
unchanged, and for a softmax classifier such changes span nearly all of
activation space. The Fisher information matrix of the log-likelihood
with respect to the penultimate activation $\act$ has rank at most $C-1$, so a
ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 has outputs that respond to only a $9$-dimensional slice
of a $512$-dimensional feature space. The other $503$ directions push every
logit up by the same amount; the softmax undoes it; confidence and entropy
remain unchanged. Failure caused by drift along these directions is therefore
silent.

Output-invisibility and harm are distinct properties. Invisibility belongs to a
\emph{direction}: a shift along the blind subspace leaves every output
statistic unchanged. Harm is the accuracy the model actually loses on held-out
labels. A blind-subspace shift can be benign, and a change in the label
function with frozen features can reduce accuracy without any shift for
FEATHER to detect. The subspace guarantees one implication: \emph{if} harmful
drift lands there, no output-based monitor can see it. FEATHER covers that
blind spot with a calibrated signal; whether a movement is harmful we
\emph{measure} from held-out labels (Sect.~\ref{sec:experiments}). No
label-free monitor can certify harm on its own.

FEATHER (Fisher-Eigenvalue Adaptive Thresholding for Error-Robust drift
detection) monitors that region directly: offline, one pass over a labeled
held-out set gives the Fisher matrix in closed form; online, a batch costs one
projection. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item{An impossibility result (Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}): the blind
    subspace is exactly the set of softmax-invariant directions, so drift kept
    inside it defeats every output-based monitor by construction.}
  \item{The FEATHER monitor: exact offline computation, millisecond online
    cost, and thresholds set to a chosen false-alarm budget.}
  \item{An evaluation in which harm is \emph{measured} from held-out labels:
    Rotated MNIST and CIFAR-10-C against output-based baselines and a
    covariance ablation, a deep-feature experiment that exposes the blind spot
    on a trained ResNet-18, and two natural shifts with real labels.}
  \item{A two-stage cascade that runs FEATHER only on batches flagged by an
    inexpensive full-space shift gate.}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\runinhead{Drift detection as change detection.}
Sequential change detection goes back to Page's CUSUM \cite{page1954cusum}.
DDM \cite{gama2004learning}, EDDM \cite{baena2006eddm}, ADWIN
\cite{bifet2007adwin} and KSWIN \cite{raab2020kswin} brought it to learning
systems, all implemented in River \cite{montiel2021river}. These
error-rate detectors tie alarms to performance but require labels, which a
stream does not provide \cite{gama2014survey, lu2018learning}. Distribution-level tests sidestep labels
with kernel two-sample statistics such as MMD \cite{gretton2012kernel} or
classifier-based tests (the covariate/label/concept taxonomy is set out in
\cite{quinonero2009dataset}, label-shift estimation in \cite{lipton2018label}),
but by construction they say nothing about performance. Rabanser et al.\
\cite{rabanser2019failing} showed that how strongly a shift is detected and how
much damage it does line up poorly, and left \emph{harmful} shift as an open
problem.

\runinhead{Confidence and label-free accuracy estimation.}
The maximum-softmax-probability baseline \cite{hendrycks2017baseline}
introduced the practice of inferring model health from its own confidence; DoC
\cite{guillory2021doc}, ATC \cite{garg2022atc} and PUDD \cite{pudd2025}
refined that estimate. Modern networks, however, are miscalibrated
\cite{guo2017calibration}, and more so under the very shift one is trying to
detect \cite{ovadia2019trust}. Closest to our work, Amoukou et al.\
\cite{amoukou2024sequential} formalize label-free sequential harmful-shift
detection with an error proxy, the framing we adopt. Every method here draws its signal from the outputs
and so inherits the blind spot of Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}: they handle
the drift the model can see, and FEATHER handles the drift it cannot.

\runinhead{Feature space and test-time adaptation.}
Working in the penultimate activation space is routine in out-of-distribution
detection: the Mahalanobis method \cite{lee2018mahalanobis} scores samples
against class-conditional Gaussians, Rabanser et al.'s strongest detector
(BBSD) tests the representations \cite{rabanser2019failing}, and Komorniczak
and Ksieniewicz \cite{komorniczak2024parallel} extract drift signals from
parallel activation patterns without labels. None of these says \emph{which}
directions the task cares about; they weight feature space by variance or
density, never by the effect on the model's decisions. Test-time adaptation
locates the shift in the representations too: Ma et al.\ \cite{ma2026pea}
correct mean, variance and covariance shift in intermediate embeddings,
on-device variants are now benchmarked \cite{danilowski2026botta}, NEO
\cite{murphy2026neo} re-centers test features by the displacement of the global
embedding centroid (exactly the full-space mean shift we use as a gate in
Sect.~\ref{sec:cascade}), and MORPHEUS \cite{danilowski2026morpheus} predicts,
without labels, how accurate each adaptation method will be from prediction
entropy and embedding geometry. These methods change the model, or
choose how to, and none separates the directions the outputs can feel from
those they cannot; FEATHER leaves the model frozen and only watches the
output-invisible subspace.

\runinhead{Fisher geometry.}
The Fisher matrix enters deep learning mainly through natural gradient
\cite{amari1998natural}, KFAC \cite{martens2015kfac}, and forgetting penalties
\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}; its spectrum concentrates in a few large
eigenvalues \cite{karakida2019universal}, a split that is exact in our
activation-space version. Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang2023score} chart the
parameter-space Fisher score vector, which requires labels, and Zero-Direction
Probing \cite{pandey2025zdp} studies null directions of activations without a
task-supervised Fisher or a harmful/benign split.

\section{Problem Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $f(x) = \softmax(W\act(x) + b)$ be a deployed, frozen $C$-class
classifier with penultimate activation $\act(x) \in \R^d$,
$W \in \R^{C \times d}$, $b \in \R^C$. Clean reference activations follow a
distribution $P_0$; in deployment an unlabeled batch $B_t$ follows a possibly
shifted $P_t$. Labels arrive once, offline, with a held-out set
$\{(\act_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$, and never on the stream. The analysis rests on one
elementary property: for any $c \in \R$,
\begin{equation}
  \softmax(z + c\,\mathbf{1}) = \softmax(z),
  \label{eq:invariance}
\end{equation}
so any activation perturbation $u$ with $Wu \propto \mathbf{1}$ is invisible in
the outputs, one sample at a time. A shift is \emph{output-invisible} when its
activation displacement lies in the blind subspace $\mathcal{N}$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:blind}), so that every output statistic is pointwise
unchanged; it is \emph{harmful} when it lowers accuracy on held-out labels past
a fixed margin and \emph{benign} when it does not (margins in
Sect.~\ref{sec:experiments}). FEATHER targets drift that is output-invisible
\emph{and} harmful, the one regime no output-based monitor can enter.

\section{The FEATHER Monitor}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The Activation-Space Fisher Matrix and Its Blind Subspace}
\label{sec:blind}

We take as monitoring object the empirical Fisher information of the
log-likelihood \emph{with respect to the activation},
\begin{equation}
  \F \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} g_i g_i^\top,
  \qquad
  g_i \;=\; \nabla_{\act}\log p(y_i \mid \act_i)
        \;=\; W^\top\!\big(e_{y_i} - p_i\big),
  \label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
with $p_i = \softmax(W\act_i + b)$ and $e_y$ the one-hot vector. Every $g_i$
lies in $W^\top\!\mathcal{H}$, where
$\mathcal{H} = \{v \in \R^C : \mathbf{1}^\top v = 0\}$ (the residual
$e_y - p$ sums to zero), so
\begin{equation}
  \rank \F \;\le\; \min\{\rank W,\; C-1\} \;\ll\; d .
  \label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
Writing $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_d$ for the eigenvalues of $\F$ with
orthonormal eigenvectors $u_j$, we call
$\mathcal{S} = \spn\{u_j : \lambda_j > \tau\lambda_1\}$ the \emph{sensitive}
subspace and $\mathcal{N} = \mathcal{S}^\perp$ the \emph{blind} subspace. The
rank gap makes the split insensitive to $\tau$: in our ResNet-18 runs the
spectrum drops by fourteen orders of magnitude after $\lambda_9$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:geomchecks}). $\F$ is the empirical rather than the true Fisher \cite{kunstner2019empirical},
but the true Fisher's per-sample covariance $\diag(p) - pp^\top$ also has rows
summing to zero, so (\ref{eq:rank}) and $\mathcal{N}$ are identical for both.

\subsection{Online Statistics and Calibration}
\label{sec:stats}

Let $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be the reference activation mean and $B_\mathcal{N}$
an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal{N}$. For a batch with shift
$\Delta\mu_t = \mu_t - \mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$ we keep three numbers: the
direction ratio
$s_t = \lVert B_\mathcal{N}^\top \Delta\mu_t\rVert / \lVert \Delta\mu_t\rVert$,
the fraction of the shift that lies in the blind subspace; the
magnitude $m_t = \lVert B_\mathcal{N}^\top \Delta\mu_t\rVert$, which
measures whether that shift is significant (by itself $s_t$ is scale-free and
would flag even small noise that happens to point into $\mathcal{N}$); and the
energy
\begin{equation}
  v_t = \frac{1}{|B_t|}\sum_{x \in B_t}
        \big\lVert B_\mathcal{N}^\top(\act(x) - \mu_{\mathrm{ref}})\big\rVert^2,
\end{equation}
which captures drift that enters $\mathcal{N}$ without shifting the mean.
The thresholds for $m_t$ and $v_t$ are quantiles of their bootstrap
distribution over clean reference batches (500 batches, 99th percentile), which
makes the false-alarm rate a chosen parameter rather than a tuned constant.
The reference data serves two purposes that must use disjoint samples: a
\emph{geometry} split fits the Fisher basis, and a disjoint \emph{calibration}
split supplies $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and the thresholds; calibrating on the fitting
split leaks the fit into the thresholds and makes them optimistic.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:feather} assembles the parts.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{FEATHER}
\label{alg:feather}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Offline} (once; split the labeled reference set into disjoint
       geometry and calibration halves):
\State on the geometry half, compute $g_i = W^\top(e_{y_i} - p_i)$ and
       $\F = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i g_i g_i^\top$
\State eigendecompose $\F$; split at the rank gap into
       $\mathcal{S} \oplus \mathcal{N}$; store basis $B_\mathcal{N}$
\State on the calibration half, set the reference mean $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and
       bootstrap thresholds $\theta_m, \theta_v$ at the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile
       of $m, v$
\Statex \textbf{Online} (per unlabeled batch $B_t$):
\State $\Delta\mu_t \gets \mathrm{mean}_{x \in B_t}\,\act(x) - \mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$
\State compute $s_t, m_t, v_t$ from $B_\mathcal{N}^\top$ projections
\State \textbf{alarm} if $m_t > \theta_m$ or $v_t > \theta_v$;
       report $s_t$ as the blind fraction of the shift
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:span}
The reference gradients $\{g_i\}$ span $W^\top\!\mathcal{H}$. This holds
almost surely once $C$ generic samples are seen; empirically we observe the
full rank $C-1$ in every run.
\end{assumption}

In a rank-deficient head ($\rank W < C-1$) the sensitive subspace only shrinks
and the blind subspace only grows. If a class is absent from, or very rare in,
the reference set, the empirical rank falls below $C-1$ and $\mathcal{N}$
becomes a \emph{superset} of the softmax-invariant set below. This is
conservative: no blind direction is missed, and the extra directions are
output-visible ones the output baselines already cover.

\begin{proposition}[Detector blindness]
\label{prop:blind}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:span},
\[
  \mathcal{N} \;=\; \{\, u \in \R^d \;:\; Wu \in \spn\{\mathbf{1}\} \,\},
\]
the set of softmax-invariant directions. Any drift that only translates
activations within $\mathcal{N}$ leaves the model's outputs pointwise
unchanged, and with them every monitoring statistic computed from outputs:
confidence, entropy, prediction histograms, DoC, ATC, uncertainty indices,
and error proxies trained on outputs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathcal{S} = \spn\{g_i\} = W^\top\!\mathcal{H}$ by the assumption, so
$u \in \mathcal{N}$ iff $(Wu)^\top v = 0$ for all $v \in \mathcal{H}$, i.e.\
iff $Wu \in \mathcal{H}^\perp = \spn\{\mathbf{1}\}$. For such $u$,
$W(\act + tu) + b = (W\act + b) + tc\,\mathbf{1}$ for some $c$, and by
(\ref{eq:invariance}) the softmax is unchanged. Outputs are unchanged input
by input, hence so is any functional of them. \qed
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} is an impossibility result about output-based
monitoring, not a claim that blind drift is harmful; Sect.~\ref{sec:experiments}
measures harm instead of assuming it. It also shows that $\mathcal{N}$ is fixed
by $W$ alone; computing it through $\F$ checks the rank structure on the
trained model.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\runinhead{Models and data.}
A small CNN ($d = 128$) trained on MNIST reaches $98.96 \pm 0.05\%$ test
accuracy, and a CIFAR-adapted ResNet-18 ($d = 512$) trained on CIFAR-10
reaches $94.14 \pm 0.20\%$; we run five seeds per model. The drift streams are
the MNIST test set rotated from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ in $15^\circ$ steps and
CIFAR-10-C \cite{hendrycks2019benchmarking} (19 corruptions $\times$ 5
severities, plus clean), streamed in batches of 500. An \emph{episode} is one
corruption-severity pair, or one angle, for one seed: 480 CIFAR and 35 MNIST
episodes. Every figure and table is regenerated from logged runs by a repository script.

\runinhead{Ground truth.}
We tag episodes by their \emph{measured} accuracy drop against the clean
episode, benign below 2 points, harmful above 10, gray in between, rather than
by the name of the corruption: 62 benign, 127 gray, 291 harmful CIFAR episodes.
The split matters: fog at severity 1 is benign while fog at severity 5 costs
more than 30 points.

\runinhead{Detectors.}
FEATHER; a covariance ablation (the same pipeline with $\F$ swapped for the
activation covariance at the same blind dimension, which isolates the choice
of subspace); and four output-based baselines: mean max-softmax confidence
\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}, mean prediction entropy, an ATC-style accuracy
estimate \cite{garg2022atc}, and a logistic error proxy after
\cite{amoukou2024sequential}. We also score the simple full-space mean shift
$\lVert\Delta\mu_t\rVert$, the representation-space analogue of a two-sample
test on the feature mean and the centroid displacement NEO \cite{murphy2026neo}
re-centers by; FEATHER is that same mean shift restricted to the blind
subspace. Every monitor alarms above the 99th percentile of its statistic's
bootstrap distribution; FEATHER alarms on either of two statistics, so its
nominal false-alarm rate is up to $2\%$, against $1\%$ for the others.

\subsection{Geometry Checks}
\label{sec:geomchecks}

On two-dimensional two-Gaussian streams whose Bayes boundary is known in closed
form, the implementation recovers the blind eigenvector to
$|\cos| > 1 - 10^{-6}$. Blind drift of four standard deviations moves the
outputs by less than $10^{-10}$ and leaves the accuracy of the frozen Bayes
classifier unchanged ($93.3\%$ before, $93.2\%$ after), yet is flagged on all 15
post-onset batches; sensitive drift, which cuts accuracy to $53.5\%$, is not
flagged and is left to the output monitors. Here $\act = x$, so blind drift is
benign by construction: the testbed checks the geometry and the detector, not
harm, which is measured separately (Sect.~\ref{sec:blinddeep}). On the
trained networks the rank bound (\ref{eq:rank}) appears exactly: in all ten
runs, exactly nine eigenvalues clear machine precision, and on a representative
CIFAR-10 seed $\lambda_9 = 9.1 \times 10^{-5}$ while
$\lambda_{10} = 5 \times 10^{-19}$. The blind subspace is thus 503 of 512
dimensions ($119$ of $128$ for the MNIST CNN).

\subsection{Blind-Subspace Shift Tracks Measured Harm}
\label{sec:saturation}

On Rotated MNIST accuracy falls from 99\% to 13\% as the angle grows, and the
FEATHER score rises monotonically with it. On CIFAR-10-C (Fig.~\ref{fig:cifar})
the blind-shift score tracks the measured accuracy drop. At the episode level, though, these benchmarks do not separate the
detectors: FEATHER, the covariance ablation, and the confidence and entropy
baselines all pull harmful from benign at AUROC $1.0$ on both benchmarks and
every seed. Corruption suites produce \emph{output-visible} drift; what separates
the monitors is how often they alarm on drift that does no harm
(Sect.~\ref{sec:alarms}).

\subsection{Deep-Feature Silent Drift}
\label{sec:blinddeep}

The benchmarks so far never reach drift the outputs \emph{cannot} express,
the regime in which only FEATHER can respond. We construct it on the trained
ResNet-18's saved activations: onto a clean batch we add a shift $\alpha u$
along a unit direction $u$ from $\mathcal{N}$ or from $\mathcal{S}$. To
measure harm, the ground-truth label depends on the sample's coordinate
along $u$, which the frozen head discards, and flips once a shifted sample
leaves its clean range; the correct label changes while every logit stays
fixed.

The contrast is unambiguous (Table~\ref{tab:blinddeep}, five seeds,
$\alpha=0.3$). A blind-subspace shift large enough to trigger FEATHER on every
batch moves the most-changed softmax probability by under $2\times10^{-10}$,
and mean confidence and entropy by under $10^{-12}$, yet pushes the synthetic
accuracy from $99\%$ down to $7\%$: harmful drift no confidence-, entropy-, or
proxy-based monitor can react to. Under the sensitive-subspace control the
outputs move and FEATHER stays at its $2\%$ floor. The plain mean shift, at the same budget, detects only $41\%$ of
blind-drift batches at $\alpha=0.3$, and $5\%$ at $\alpha=0.2$, where accuracy
has already fallen to $43\%$. Clean-batch noise in $\Delta\mu$ is concentrated
in the high-variance, class-discriminative sensitive directions; FEATHER
projects them out, which lowers its threshold by a factor of about $3.4$
($0.11$ vs.\ $0.37$). Figure~\ref{fig:blinddeep} follows these effects as
$\alpha$ grows.
Against the real labels of these reference images, accuracy stays at
$99.97\%$ in both directions: a shift that leaves every output unchanged cannot change
correctness against fixed labels, so the harm here exists only under the
synthetic rule. The experiment establishes output-invisibility and FEATHER
detection on a real network, not harm on natural data.

\subsection{Where the False Alarms Land}
\label{sec:alarms}

Table~\ref{tab:alarms} sets all seven monitors against the same CIFAR-10-C
episodes. The output baselines are calibrated on a held-out split of the clean
deployment stream; without that they alarm on every clean test batch, owing
to the train-to-test confidence gap. Because this drift is output-visible, the
output baselines perform best here ($6$--$9\%$ benign alarms), just as
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} predicts. FEATHER alarms more often
($47.6 \pm 6.9\%$), since a benign corruption can move the blind subspace while
sparing accuracy. These alarms are sustained, not noise: a
three-consecutive-batch run rule lowers FEATHER's benign rate only to
$0.32 \pm 0.07$ (the output baselines' to at most $0.01$). Against its own covariance ablation, however, FEATHER is
clearly superior: the ablation alarms on $82.9 \pm 6.9\%$ of benign batches, a gap of
$35.3 \pm 3.6$ points that stays positive on every seed (an exact paired
sign-flip test gives $p = 0.0625$, the smallest this design can produce).
Low-variance directions move under benign corruption and even clean
resampling (the ablation's $28\%$ clean rate); low-Fisher directions do not. The full-space mean shift alarms on fewer benign batches
than FEATHER ($0.20 \pm 0.03$, lower on every seed), but for the same reason it
misses blind drift: its larger threshold makes it less sensitive overall
(Sect.~\ref{sec:blinddeep}). The result also holds at matched calibration: recalibrating
FEATHER on a held-out split of the clean \emph{test} stream, as the output
baselines are, keeps its clean false-alarm rate at $0.10 \pm 0.06$ on
CIFAR-10-C and $0.08 \pm 0.04$ on MNIST, and still alarms on fewer benign
shifts than the ablation ($0.59 \pm 0.05$ vs.\ $0.67 \pm 0.05$) while both
detect every harmful one.

\subsection{Natural Shift on Real Data}
\label{sec:wilds}

\runinhead{Hospital shift.} To test a real distribution shift with real labels we use WILDS Camelyon17
\cite{koh2021wilds}, a binary tumour-vs-normal patch task whose domains are
hospitals. A ResNet-18 trained on the in-distribution hospitals ($98.8\%$
validation accuracy) is monitored as the two held-out hospitals stream in. As
the rank bound predicts for a binary task, one Fisher eigenvalue
($1.5\times10^{-2}$) sits above a cliff to $\sim10^{-18}$, leaving $511$ of
$512$ dimensions blind. The hospital shift does real harm --- accuracy falls from $99.0\%$
in distribution to $69.5\%$ and $58.5\%$ on the two out-of-domain hospitals ---
and FEATHER tracks it, staying within its budget in distribution and alarming
on every batch of both shifted hospitals. The output baselines alarm on all
shifted batches too: stain and morphology changes are output-visible.

\runinhead{Subpopulation shift.} Harm with unchanged outputs requires new
inputs with their own labels, so we pre-registered a BREEDS-style shift
\cite{santurkar2021breeds}: a ResNet-18 trained on the 20 CIFAR-100
superclasses using four of the five subclasses of each ($83.0 \pm 0.8\%$
accuracy) monitors streams in which a fraction $\rho$ of each batch comes from
the held-out subclasses. The pre-registered criterion, a drop of at least ten
points flagged by FEATHER while every output baseline stays below $20\%$
alarms, was not met. The output baselines, calibrated as in
Sect.~\ref{sec:alarms}, reach $50\%$ alarms at $\rho = 0.2$ (a $7.8$-point
drop); FEATHER, calibrated on the training reference ($7\%$ clean false
alarms), at $\rho = 0.3$ ($12.0$ points); the mean shift only at $\rho = 0.8$.
Across 20 single-superclass swaps the drop correlates with the output scores
(Spearman $0.85$--$0.87$) and negatively with FEATHER's blind fraction $s$
($-0.86$): harmful subclasses shift mainly along the sensitive directions. This
shift, too, is output-visible.

\subsection{Cost and a Two-Stage Cascade}
\label{sec:cascade}

For the ResNet-18 the offline fit, including the forward passes over
50{,}000 images, takes 11.1 s; online, FEATHER's statistics take 3.0 ms per
500-image batch on a laptop CPU, small next to the forward pass. The
$O(nd^2 + d^3)$ fit remains seconds at $d \approx 4096$
\cite{halko2011structure}. Since FEATHER's statistic is the blind part of
$\lVert\Delta\mu\rVert$, the full-space shift (0.05 ms) serves as a gate, and
the projection runs only when it alarms. On
CIFAR-10-C the cascade matches FEATHER-always on harmful episodes ($0.80$
vs.\ $0.82$ of batches) and alarms on $16\%$ of benign batches against $40\%$;
on Rotated MNIST the gate fires on almost every batch, so it saves nothing.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

On corruption suites, whose drift is output-visible, FEATHER does not beat
the output monitors on false alarms; in the silent regime of
Sect.~\ref{sec:blinddeep} it is the only effective one. The two are
complementary: a confidence monitor for visible failures, FEATHER for blind
ones. Choosing the subspace by the Fisher spectrum rather than by variance
nearly halves the covariance ablation's benign alarms,
since the discarded low-Fisher directions are those the decisions ignore; the
plain mean shift has fewer benign alarms but a threshold too high to catch
blind drift before accuracy collapses. Benchmarks
that include the silent regime by design are needed to rank label-free
monitors.

Pure concept shift that leaves the activation marginals alone is invisible to
every label-free method, ours included. The
silent-regime result is semi-synthetic (drift injected in activation space,
harm from a synthetic rule), and both natural shifts we tested, the WILDS hospital shift and the
pre-registered subpopulation shift, are output-visible; a natural shift that
brings in differently labelled inputs whose outputs match the reference
remains the most valuable next step. Held-out calibration holds the clean
false-alarm rate at budget only when the reference data matches the deployment
stream: calibrated on its training split, FEATHER alarms on every clean MNIST
test batch, and on $8\%$ once recalibrated on clean test data.
Finally, the blind subspace belongs to the penultimate layer; an
intermediate-layer version through a Kronecker-factored Fisher
\cite{martens2015kfac} would capture drift that vanishes before it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The geometry of a softmax head makes the price of output-based monitoring
exact: the outputs are a $(C-1)$-dimensional window onto a $d$-dimensional
representation, and everything orthogonal to it is invisible. FEATHER computes
the invisible part exactly and monitors it at the cost of a single projection. It
follows measured harm, nearly halves the false alarms of its covariance
ablation, and, in the semi-synthetic setting, is the only monitor that detects
harmful drift the outputs cannot express, complementing confidence-based tools in the one region they provably
cannot reach.
