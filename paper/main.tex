\begin{abstract}
Label-free monitors for deployed classifiers --- confidence tracking, entropy,
black-box shift detection, and learned accuracy estimators --- share one
structural weakness: their signal is a function of the model's outputs. We show
that every such monitor has a formally characterizable blind spot. The Fisher
information matrix of the log-likelihood with respect to a classifier's
penultimate activations has rank at most $C-1$ for a $C$-class softmax head;
its orthogonal complement --- the \emph{blind subspace} --- consists exactly of
the activation directions that change all logits equally, and drift confined to
it leaves the model's output distribution unchanged. Consequently no
output-based statistic can ever detect it, while the environment producing the
activations may have changed arbitrarily. We propose FEATHER, a streaming
monitor that computes this subspace exactly and in closed form from a single
labeled reference pass, then watches it online with three
bootstrap-calibrated statistics at the cost of one matrix--vector product per
batch. A cascaded variant, FEATHER-Lite, combines a classical statistical
detector with FEATHER's geometric filter. On controlled two-dimensional
streams the implementation recovers the theoretical blind subspace to
machine precision, verifies output-invariance of blind drift to $10^{-10}$,
and detects every post-onset blind-drift batch at a calibrated false-alarm
rate. Experiments on Rotated~MNIST and CIFAR-10-C
\todo{complete once Tier 2--3 experiments run} evaluate how often
blind-subspace drift coincides with silent accuracy degradation in deep
networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Machine learning systems degrade after deployment because the world that
produces their inputs changes --- a phenomenon known as \emph{concept drift}.
Production monitoring is constrained by two facts. First, ground-truth labels
arrive late or never, so accuracy cannot be tracked directly. Second, not
every distribution change matters: many shifts are \emph{benign} (accuracy is
unaffected), some are \emph{harmful} (accuracy collapses), and a monitor that
alarms on every statistical change drowns operators in false positives
\citep{rabanser2019failing}.

The modern response is label-free performance estimation: tracking softmax
confidence, prediction entropy, differences of confidences
\citep{guillory2021doc}, learned confidence thresholds \citep{garg2022atc},
prediction-uncertainty detectors \citep{pudd2025}, or error-proxy models with
sequential testing \citep{amoukou2024sequential}. These methods are effective
when drift moves the model's outputs. Our starting observation is that this
entire family shares a single structural property: \textbf{their signal is a
function of the model's outputs, so any drift that leaves the outputs
unchanged is invisible to all of them --- by construction, not by
misfortune.}

Such drift exists and is easy to characterize. For a classifier with a softmax
head, the Fisher information matrix (FIM) of the log-likelihood with respect
to the \emph{penultimate activation} has rank at most $C-1$ ($C$ classes);
in a typical deep network the activation dimension $d$ is hundreds, so almost
all of activation space lies in the orthogonal complement. We show this
complement consists exactly of the directions that shift all logits equally
--- directions to which the softmax is invariant. Activations can move
arbitrarily far along them while confidence, entropy, and every
prediction-derived statistic remain frozen. If the world changed in a way the
model cannot react to, the model fails \emph{silently}.

FEATHER (Fisher-Eigenvalue Adaptive Thresholding for Error-Robust drift
detection) monitors exactly this blind region. Offline, on a labeled
reference set, it computes the $d \times d$ activation-space FIM
\emph{exactly} --- no Kronecker-factored approximation is required, in
contrast to parameter-space Fisher methods \citep{martens2015kfac} --- and
eigendecomposes it into a \emph{sensitive} subspace (where outputs react) and
a \emph{blind} subspace (where they provably do not). Online, each incoming
batch costs one matrix--vector projection; three statistics with
bootstrap-calibrated thresholds control the false-alarm rate directly.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A detector-blindness characterization} (Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}):
    the orthogonal complement of the activation-space Fisher column space is
    exactly the set of softmax-invariant activation directions, hence no
    output-based monitor can detect drift confined to it. This positions
    geometric monitoring as \emph{necessary}, not merely alternative.
  \item \textbf{FEATHER}, a label-free streaming monitor of the blind subspace:
    exact closed-form offline computation, millisecond online projections,
    three complementary statistics (direction, magnitude, energy), and
    bootstrap threshold calibration; plus \textbf{FEATHER-Lite}, a cascade
    with a classical statistical first stage.
  \item \textbf{A controlled validation} in which every quantity has an
    analytical ground truth, confirming the theory to machine precision, and
    an evaluation protocol on Rotated~MNIST and CIFAR-10-C
    \citep{hendrycks2019benchmarking} in which harmfulness is \emph{measured}
    from held-out labels rather than assumed from corruption type.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Statistical drift detection.}
Classical stream-mining detectors test for change in a scalar stream:
ADWIN's adaptive windowing \citep{bifet2007adwin}, DDM-style error-rate
monitoring, Kolmogorov--Smirnov windows, and kernel two-sample tests.
Error-rate detectors require labels; distribution detectors are
performance-agnostic and alarm on benign change. \citet{rabanser2019failing}
showed empirically that detection strength correlates poorly with damage and
posed harmful-shift characterization as an open problem.

\paragraph{Label-free performance estimation.}
DoC \citep{guillory2021doc} and ATC \citep{garg2022atc} estimate target
accuracy from confidence statistics; PUDD \citep{pudd2025} detects drift from
prediction uncertainty. \citet{amoukou2024sequential} formalize
\emph{sequential harmful shift detection without labels} via an error-proxy
model and sequential testing, establishing the problem framing we adopt.
All of these read the model's outputs (or models trained to mimic them) and
therefore inherit the blind spot formalized in
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}; they are natural baselines and complements, not
competitors, to a blind-subspace monitor.

\paragraph{Fisher geometry of neural networks.}
The FIM appears in deep learning chiefly as an optimization and compression
object \citep{martens2015kfac}. \citet{karakida2019universal} showed that
deep-network Fisher spectra concentrate: a few large eigenvalues and a
near-zero bulk --- i.e., large near-null subspaces exist. Two works bring
related ideas to monitoring: \citet{zhang2023score} track the Fisher
\emph{score vector} with control charts (a detection signal, not a
harmfulness criterion, and a different object from the FIM eigenstructure),
and Zero-Direction Probing \citep{pandey2025zdp} uses activation null
directions to study representational drift in large language models
(theory-only, no task-supervised Fisher, no harmful/benign separation).
FEATHER occupies the gap between \citet{amoukou2024sequential} (the framing,
without geometry) and \citet{pandey2025zdp} (the geometry, without the
deployment problem).

\section{Method}

\subsection{Setup and notation}

Let $f(x) = \softmax(W\act(x) + b)$ be a deployed, frozen $C$-class
classifier, where $\act(x) \in \R^d$ is the penultimate activation,
$W \in \R^{C \times d}$, $b \in \R^C$. Data arrive in batches
$B_t = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^{n_t}$. A labeled reference set
$\{(\act_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ (held-out training data) is available
\emph{offline only}; the stream itself is unlabeled.

\subsection{The activation-space Fisher matrix and its blind subspace}

We define the monitoring matrix as the empirical Fisher information of the
log-likelihood \emph{with respect to the activation}:
\begin{equation}
  \F \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} g_i g_i^\top,
  \qquad
  g_i \;=\; \nabla_{\act}\log p(y_i \mid \act_i)
        \;=\; W^\top\!\big(e_{y_i} - p_i\big),
  \label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
where $p_i = \softmax(W\act_i + b)$ and $e_y$ is the one-hot vector. Since
every $g_i$ lies in $W^\top\!\mathcal{H}$ with
$\mathcal{H} = \{v \in \R^C : \mathbf{1}^\top v = 0\}$ (the residual
$e_y - p$ always sums to zero),
\begin{equation}
  \rank \F \;\le\; \min\{\rank W,\; C-1\} \;\ll\; d .
\end{equation}
$\F$ is a $d \times d$ matrix computed \emph{exactly} in one pass over the
reference set and eigendecomposed in milliseconds; no approximation enters
the core method. Let $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_d \ge 0$ be its
eigenvalues with orthonormal eigenvectors $u_1, \dots, u_d$. We split
activation space into the \emph{sensitive} subspace
$\mathcal{S} = \spn\{u_j : \lambda_j > \tau\lambda_1\}$
and the \emph{blind} subspace $\mathcal{N} = \mathcal{S}^\perp$
(relative tolerance $\tau$; given the rank structure the split is
insensitive to $\tau$ over many orders of magnitude).

\subsection{Online statistics and calibration}
\label{sec:stats}

Let $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be the reference activation mean and $P_\mathcal{N}$
the orthogonal projector onto $\mathcal{N}$ (stored as a $d\times k$ basis
$B_\mathcal{N}$). For each batch with mean $\mu_t$ and shift
$\Delta\mu_t = \mu_t - \mu_{\mathrm{ref}}$, FEATHER computes
\begin{align}
  s_t &= \frac{\lVert B_\mathcal{N}^\top \Delta\mu_t\rVert}
              {\lVert \Delta\mu_t\rVert}
  &&\text{(direction ratio: where is the drift pointing?)} \\
  m_t &= \lVert B_\mathcal{N}^\top \Delta\mu_t\rVert
  &&\text{(shift magnitude: is it big enough to matter?)} \\
  v_t &= \frac{1}{n_t}\sum_{x \in B_t}
         \big\lVert B_\mathcal{N}^\top(\act(x) - \mu_{\mathrm{ref}})\big\rVert^2
  &&\text{(energy: catches variance-only drift).}
\end{align}
The ratio $s_t$ alone is scale-invariant and would alarm on noise-level
shifts that happen to point into $\mathcal{N}$; the magnitude $m_t$ gates it.
The energy $v_t$ detects drift that spreads into $\mathcal{N}$ without moving
the mean. Thresholds for $m_t$ and $v_t$ are set as the $q$-th quantile of
their bootstrap distributions over clean reference batches, giving a
per-statistic stationary false-alarm rate of approximately $1-q$ with no
hand-tuned constants. The online cost per batch is one $d\times k$
matrix--vector product.

\subsection{FEATHER-Lite: a cascaded detector}

FEATHER answers ``is the drift in the blind region?''; classical detectors
answer ``did anything change?'' cheaply. FEATHER-Lite composes them: a
statistical first stage (e.g.\ KSWIN or ADWIN \citep{bifet2007adwin} on a
scalar summary of activations, via the River library
\citep{montiel2021river}) flags candidate change-points with high
sensitivity; FEATHER then classifies the flagged shift as output-visible
(existing monitors and confidence-based triage apply) or blind (silent-failure
risk; escalate). Label-consuming detectors are reported only as an
upper-bound reference in our experiments.

\section{Theory}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:span}
The reference gradients $\{g_i\}$ span $W^\top\!\mathcal{H}$. (This holds
almost surely once $n \ge C$ batches of generic data are observed, and is
verified numerically in our experiments.)
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Detector blindness]
\label{prop:blind}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:span}, the blind subspace is exactly the set of
softmax-invariant directions:
\[
  \mathcal{N} \;=\; \{\, u \in \R^d \;:\; Wu \in \spn\{\mathbf{1}\} \,\}.
\]
Consequently, for any drift that only translates activations within
$\mathcal{N}$, the model's output distribution is identical to its
pre-drift value on every input, and \emph{any} monitoring statistic computed
from model outputs --- confidence, entropy, prediction histograms, DoC, ATC,
uncertainty indices, or error-proxies trained on outputs --- is invariant to
the drift.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Assumption~\ref{ass:span},
$\mathcal{S} = \spn\{g_i\} = W^\top\!\mathcal{H}$, so
$u \in \mathcal{N} = \mathcal{S}^\perp$ iff
$0 = u^\top W^\top v = (Wu)^\top v$ for all $v \in \mathcal{H}$, i.e.\ iff
$Wu \in \mathcal{H}^\perp = \spn\{\mathbf{1}\}$. For such $u$ and any
$t \in \R$, the logits of a shifted activation satisfy
$W(\act + tu) + b = (W\act + b) + tc\,\mathbf{1}$ for some $c$, and
$\softmax(z + tc\,\mathbf{1}) = \softmax(z)$. Outputs are therefore pointwise
unchanged, and so is every functional of them.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Calibrated response in the sensitive subspace]
\label{prop:bounded}
For any activation perturbation $\Delta \in \R^d$ and any class $y$,
\[
  \big|\log p(y \mid \act + \Delta) - \log p(y \mid \act)\big|
  \;\le\; 2\,\lVert W\Delta \rVert_\infty .
\]
In particular the log-likelihood response to a shift confined to
$\mathcal{S}$ is bounded and continuous in the shift magnitude, and zero for
a shift confined to $\mathcal{N}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\log p(y \mid \act) = z_y - \mathrm{logsumexp}(z)$ with
$z = W\act + b$. The first term changes by $(W\Delta)_y$, bounded by
$\lVert W\Delta\rVert_\infty$; logsumexp is $1$-Lipschitz with respect to the
$\ell_\infty$ norm, contributing at most another
$\lVert W\Delta\rVert_\infty$. For $\Delta \in \mathcal{N}$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} gives exact invariance.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation and scope.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} is an impossibility result for output-based
monitoring, not a harm guarantee: first-order geometry cannot force errors
--- blind drift is dangerous precisely because the model \emph{cannot} react
to it, so if the label function or the validity of the upstream feature
extractor changed along those directions, failure is silent. Whether
blind-subspace activation drift \emph{correlates} with silent accuracy loss
in deep networks is an empirical question, and our evaluation is designed to
measure it rather than assume it. Online eigen-tracking of a time-varying
subspace is possible via streaming decompositions \citep{halko2011structure};
we deliberately keep the subspace fixed between (cheap, exact) offline
recomputations, since it is a property of the frozen model.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Controlled validation with analytical ground truth (completed)}
\label{sec:tier1}

We first validate every component on two-dimensional, two-class Gaussian
streams with shared covariance, where the Bayes-optimal boundary --- and
hence the exact sensitive/blind split --- is known in closed form: the
sensitive direction is the boundary normal, the blind direction is
boundary-parallel (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}). Drift is injected as a
covariate shift of controllable direction, magnitude, and onset. The
implementation is fully deterministic (seeded) and covered by 38 unit and
integration tests.

\paragraph{Results.} All theoretical quantities are recovered numerically:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Subspace recovery:} the computed blind eigenvector aligns
    with the analytical boundary-parallel direction with
    $|\cos| > 1 - 10^{-6}$; the Fisher matrix is rank-one
    ($\lambda_2 \le 10^{-10}\lambda_1$), matching
    $\rank \F \le C - 1 = 1$.
  \item \textbf{Detector blindness (Prop.~\ref{prop:blind}):} under a
    blind-subspace shift of magnitude $4.0$, per-sample softmax outputs
    change by less than $10^{-10}$ --- confidence, entropy, and BBSD-class
    statistics are exactly flat (Figure~\ref{fig:demo}, top left).
  \item \textbf{Detection:} the calibrated monitor ($q = 0.99$,
    $300$ bootstrap batches) alarms on $15/15$ post-onset blind-drift
    batches with direction ratio $s_t > 0.95$, while the measured stationary
    alarm rate over $200$ clean batches stays within the calibrated budget
    ($\le 8\%$ for the union of two $1\%$-quantile statistics).
  \item \textbf{Separation:} sensitive-direction drift of magnitude $3.0$
    produces mean $s_t < 0.2$ and no blind-monitor alarms
    (Figure~\ref{fig:demo}, right column).
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

The blind subspace is a property of the frozen head; it is exact for the
softmax layer but summarizes deeper feature drift only through the
penultimate activations (a multi-layer extension via Kronecker-factored
parameter-space Fisher \citep{martens2015kfac} is a natural ablation).
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} bounds what output-based monitors can see; it
does not by itself guarantee that blind drift is harmful --- pure concept
shift with unchanged activation marginals remains undetectable for
\emph{any} label-free method, ours included. The reference window is assumed
representative; on model retraining the subspace must be recomputed (exact
and cheap). Empirical validation beyond the controlled setting is in
progress (Section~\ref{sec:tier23}).

\section{Conclusion}

Modern label-free monitoring reads a model's outputs, and we show this
creates a provable, exactly characterizable blind spot: the orthogonal
complement of the activation-space Fisher, equal to the softmax-invariant
directions. FEATHER computes that subspace in closed form and watches it in
real time with calibrated false-alarm control, turning a two-line
impossibility argument into a deployable monitor and completing the coverage
of existing detectors rather than competing with them on drift they already
see.
